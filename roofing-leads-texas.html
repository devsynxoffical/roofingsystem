Why buying Texas roofing leads stops working

Lead sellers make money when you keep buying. Here is what that costs you.

  • Shared with other roofers

    Many bought leads go to several contractors at once. The homeowner picks whoever calls first, or whoever is cheapest.

  • You pay forever

    Stop paying and the leads stop. Nothing you bought builds your brand, your pages or your customer list.

  • Storm-season crowding

    After hail, every roofer and storm chaser in the area is in the same feeds and lists. Generic offers disappear.

  • Slow first contact

    If your team calls an hour later, the homeowner has already booked someone else.

How much do roofing leads cost in Texas?

Pay-per-lead vendors list exclusive Texas roofing leads at around $55 each (for example, 99 Calls Texas roofing leads).

Our own Texas campaigns produced leads at $5.31 each (686 leads in 8 days) and $7.60 each (850 leads in 8 days). A multi-state campaign fulfilling jobs in Texas, Florida and Georgia came in at $4.27. These are different lead types: vendor leads are often pre-screened calls, ours are form leads you qualify yourself. Compare them by cost per signed job, not just cost per lead.

From leads to signed jobs in Dallas

Leads only matter if they close. In our Dallas campaign, the company closed $18,000 and 5 installs in the last two weeks of December, $75,600 and 21 installs in January, and $33,900 and 8 installs from 1 to 19 February: $127,500 and 34 installs in just over 60 days.

What made the difference was positioning the company as one of the most trusted roofers in Dallas from the first second of the ad, then a structured offer and follow-up system behind it. See how follow-up works in our lead follow-up guide.

Shared, exclusive or owned?

Shared leads are sold to several roofers. Exclusive leads are sold to one, but you still rent them. An owned system means homeowners respond to your brand, and the pages and data stay with you. The full comparison is in shared vs exclusive roofing leads.
