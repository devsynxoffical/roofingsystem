What Do Roofing Facebook Ads Cost Per Lead? Data From 15 Real Campaigns

Most published numbers on roofing Facebook ad costs are broad industry benchmarks. Here are the actual cost-per-lead results from 15 roofing campaigns we ran on Meta (Facebook and Instagram) between September 2025 and October 2026, with each campaign linked to its case study so you can check the numbers.

Short answer

Across 15 roofing Meta ad campaigns and 7,765 homeowner leads, the median cost per lead was $11.41, ranging from $3.75 to $24. Commonly cited industry benchmarks put roofing Facebook leads at roughly $40 to $80. Cost per lead is only a starting point: what matters is cost per booked inspection and per signed job.

The headline numbers

  • 15 campaigns, 7,765 leads for roofing contractors and one roofing pay-per-lead agency.
  • Median cost per lead: $11.41. Lowest $3.75 (day one of a campaign), highest $24.
  • 13 of 15 campaigns came in under $15 per lead, and 7 of 15 under $10.
  • Blended CPL $11.98 across the 9 campaigns that also reported total spend ($50,853 spent, 4,244 leads).

For comparison, published roofing benchmarks commonly cluster around $40 to $80 per lead on Facebook (see the WebTonic roofing Meta ads statistics report and JobNimbus on when Facebook ads work for roofers). Those are averages across very different accounts, so treat the gap as a sign of what a structured system can do, not a promise.

What the lowest-CPL campaigns had in common

Reading across the case studies, the same few factors come up again and again:

  1. 01A specific offer, not "free inspection". The Texas roofer that went from zero leads to 686 in 8 days switched to a $0-down, 0%-interest-for-12-months financing offer. (Financing claims need the lender's terms and the required disclosures.)
  2. 02Creative that earns the click. Most low-CPL campaigns held a click-through rate above 2%, and one reached 3.79%. A higher CTR usually means cheaper leads on Meta.
  3. 03Targeting built around intent. Audience segmentation and intent layering instead of one broad audience. We cover this in the free audience segmentation training.
  4. 04Lead verification. Several campaigns used OTP (one-time passcode) verification, so every counted lead had a real, reachable phone number. Verified leads usually cost a little more but waste less sales time.
  5. 05Speed. Campaigns were launched and adjusted fast; the brand-new account with no pixel history still reached $14.80 per lead in its first week.

Why cost per lead is not the number to optimize

A $5 lead that never answers the phone is more expensive than a $20 lead that books an inspection. The figure that pays the bills is cost per signed job. In the clearest example we have, a Dallas roofer turned its campaign into $127,500 in closed deals and 34 installs in about 60 days. That outcome depended as much on fast follow-up and qualification as on the ad cost.

Definitions

Cost per lead (CPL): ad spend divided by the number of leads. OTP-verified lead: a lead who confirmed their phone number with a one-time passcode. CTR: the share of people who saw the ad and clicked it. CPM: the cost of 1,000 ad impressions.

How to read this data

  • These are our own campaigns, reported in the original posts; we have not mixed in third-party data.
  • 15 campaigns is a small sample, and markets, offers and seasons differ. Your results will differ too: see our income disclosure.
  • Lead definitions vary: some campaigns count form fills, others only OTP-verified or pre-qualified leads.
  • Most campaigns report results from the first days or weeks; costs can rise or fall as campaigns mature.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Still have a question? Ask us on a free call or read the full FAQ.

What is a good cost per lead for roofing Facebook ads?+

Across our 15 roofing Meta campaigns the median was $11.41 per lead, and 13 of 15 were under $15. Industry benchmarks commonly cited for roofing Facebook leads are around $40 to $80. A good CPL is one that turns into booked inspections and signed jobs at a profitable cost.

Why are some roofing leads so much cheaper than others?+

In our campaigns the lowest costs came with a specific offer (such as $0-down financing), creative with a click-through rate above 2%, intent-based audience targeting and fast launch and iteration.

Are cheap roofing leads lower quality?+

Not necessarily, but cheap leads can be unqualified. Verification (such as OTP phone verification), qualification questions and fast follow-up decide whether low-cost leads turn into appointments.

Is cost per lead the best way to measure roofing ads?+

No. Cost per lead is a starting point; cost per booked inspection and cost per signed job show whether the ads are profitable.
