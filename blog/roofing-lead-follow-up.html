Roofing sales follow-up

How to Follow Up on Roofing Leads (and Book More Inspections)

Most roofing leads are not lost to a competitor's better price — they are lost to silence. The homeowner fills in a form, waits, gets busy, and books with whoever reached them first. Here is a follow-up process you can run on every lead, whatever the source.

Short answer

Respond to every roofing lead within minutes with an automatic text and email, call as soon as possible, qualify before driving out, book the inspection on the first conversation, and keep following up by call, text and email until the homeowner books or says no.

1. Respond immediately — every time

The first response should happen within minutes, not at the end of the day. A homeowner who just asked about a roof is thinking about it right now; an hour later they are back at work, and the next roofer who calls gets the conversation.

  • Send an automatic text and email the moment a lead arrives, confirming you received the request and saying when someone will call.
  • Call as soon as possible after that. If nobody answers, leave a short voicemail and follow with a text.
  • Route new leads to whoever is available, rather than to one person's inbox.

2. Qualify before you drive out

An inspection costs your team time and fuel. A few questions up front protect that time and make the appointment more useful for the homeowner:

  • Do they own the home, and is the property inside your service area?
  • What prompted the request — a leak, storm damage, an older roof, selling the home?
  • Is there an insurance claim involved, or is it a retail job?
  • Who else needs to be at the appointment to make a decision?
  • What timeline are they working to?

3. Book the inspection on the first conversation

Offer two or three specific time slots instead of asking "when works for you?". Confirm by text straight away, and send a reminder the day before and on the morning of the appointment. Make rescheduling easy — a homeowner who can move the appointment is less likely to simply not show up.

4. Keep following up after no answer

Many leads go quiet rather than say no. One unanswered call is not a decision. Set up a sequence that mixes calls, texts and emails over the following days and weeks, and stop only when the homeowner books, says no, or asks you to stop.

  1. 01Day 1: instant text and email, first call, voicemail and follow-up text if unanswered.
  2. 02Days 2–3: another call at a different time of day, plus a short helpful text (for example, what to expect at an inspection).
  3. 03First week: an email with useful information — photos of common roof issues, how insurance inspections work in your area, your warranty.
  4. 04Following weeks: a lighter-touch check-in by text or email until the homeowner responds.

Keep it helpful

Follow-up works when each message gives the homeowner something useful or makes the next step easier. Ten messages of "just checking in" will be ignored; a reminder about a storm-damage inspection window will not.

5. Follow up after the estimate, too

Homeowners often collect several estimates before choosing. The roofer who follows up clearly — answering questions, explaining the scope, making it easy to say yes — is often the one who wins, even without the lowest price. Put post-estimate follow-up in the same system so it never depends on someone remembering.

6. Track everything in a CRM

If follow-up lives in someone's head or a notebook, it will slip in busy weeks — exactly when you have the most leads. A CRM shows every lead's status, who contacted them and when, and which leads need action today. It also tells you which lead sources produce booked jobs, not just form fills.

Real example

In a 16-day Florida campaign across 4 locations, every one of 365 OTP-verified leads was synced instantly to the CRM, nurtured by SMS and email, and routed to sales: follow-up running automatically, not from memory.

Automating the process

Most of the steps above can run automatically: instant replies, reminders, follow-up sequences and pipeline updates. Your team then spends its time on the conversations that need a person. FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Still have a question? Ask us on a free call or read the full FAQ.

How fast should I respond to a roofing lead?+

Within minutes. Send an automatic text and email immediately, then call as soon as possible. The longer a lead waits, the more likely the homeowner has spoken to another roofer or moved on.

How many times should I follow up with a roofing lead?+

Keep following up with a mix of calls, texts and emails until the homeowner books, says no, or asks you to stop. Many leads go quiet rather than decline, so a single unanswered call is not a decision.

Should roofers use a CRM for follow-up?+

Yes. A CRM keeps every lead's status and history in one place, makes sure nothing is missed in busy weeks, and shows which lead sources turn into booked jobs.
