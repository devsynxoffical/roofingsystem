The problem

Why choosing a lead company is hard

Most comparisons are written by the vendors themselves. Watch for these traps.

  • Shared without saying so

    Some leads go to several contractors at once, which turns every call into a race on speed and price.

  • Hidden fees

    Membership, subscription or profile fees on top of the per-lead price raise the real cost.

  • Vague quality promises

    "Qualified" and "guaranteed" mean different things to each vendor. Read the refund terms.

  • Nothing you keep

    With most lead sellers, the pages, ads and data stay with the seller when you stop paying.

The options

Four types of roofing lead generation companies

Each type suits a different roofing company. Details and sources are in the sections below.

  • Shared lead marketplacesAngi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack and similar sites. Fast to start; leads are often shared with other pros.
  • Exclusive lead vendorsCompanies such as 99 Calls that sell each lead to one contractor, priced per lead.
  • Google Local Services AdsGoogle's own pay-per-lead listings at the top of local results, exclusive to your company.
  • Owned lead systemsAgencies and partners that run ads, pages and follow-up under your brand, such as Roofing Systems Co.

Buying leads vs owning the system

Both get you homeowner contacts. Only one builds an asset your company keeps.

Buying leads

  • Fast to start
  • Paid per lead, every month
  • Often shared or resold
  • The vendor keeps pages and data

Owning the system

  • Takes setup
  • You pay ad spend plus management
  • Exclusive to your brand
  • You keep ads, pages, CRM and data

Shared lead marketplaces: Angi, HomeAdvisor and Thumbtack

Angi and HomeAdvisor are part of the same company and match homeowners with local pros. Neither publishes a fixed roofing lead price; cost depends on the market and job type, some plans add membership or profile fees, and the same homeowner request can reach more than one pro. Thumbtack also charges per lead (WebFX comparison of Local Services Ads and Thumbtack).

Best for: roofers who want volume quickly and can call within minutes. Watch for: shared leads and extra fees.

Exclusive lead vendors: 99 Calls and Modernize

99 Calls sells exclusive roofing leads and lists them at under $50 per lead on its roofing page (99 Calls roofing leads). Modernize sells roofing leads with prices that vary by trade, intent, volume and market, and does not publish a fixed price (Modernize lead costs).

Best for: roofers who want exclusive leads without running ads. Watch for: how leads are generated, refund terms and volume limits.

Google Local Services Ads

Local Services Ads appear above regular Google results with a Google screening badge. You pay when a homeowner contacts you through the ad, not per click, and the lead goes to your company only (WebFX on Local Services Ads). Reviews and responsiveness strongly affect how often you show. See Google Ads for roofers.

Best for: roofers with strong reviews who can answer calls fast. Watch for: competition in large metros and limited control over volume.

Owned lead systems, including Roofing Systems Co.

An owned system runs ads under your company name, sends homeowners to your own pages, verifies phone numbers and follows up from your CRM. Roofing Systems Co. works this way: ad spend goes to Meta in your account, management is quoted after a strategy call, work is backed by a written 90-day agreement with no long-term lock-in, and you keep the ads, pages, CRM and data. Across our published roofing campaigns, cost per lead ranged from $3.75 to $24 (cost report).

Best for: roofing companies doing $1M+ that want exclusive leads and an asset they keep. Watch for: setup time before the first campaign, and the need to follow up fast.

How to choose a roofing lead generation company

1. Are the leads exclusive, and how is that defined in writing? 2. How are leads generated and verified? 3. What do you pay beyond the per-lead price? 4. Can they show roofing results with numbers? 5. What are the refund or replacement terms? 6. Who owns the pages, ads and data if you stop?
